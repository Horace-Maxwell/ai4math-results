\begin{abstract}
A graph is integral if its adjacency eigenvalues are integers, and a
generalized sun graph is a cycle with pendant paths attached to its vertices.
Braga, Del-Vecchio and Rodrigues conjectured that the
cycle of an integral generalized sun graph that is not a cycle has length
divisible by $4$. Braga, Moraes and Santos disproved this with the graph
$C_{6,1}(0,6,6,12,6,6)$ on $42$ vertices, and remarked that their computer
search does not show that no smaller counterexample exists. We show that
none exists: up to isomorphism, $C_{6,1}(0,6,6,12,6,6)$ is the only
counterexample with at most $42$ vertices, also when pendant paths of
different lengths share a vertex. Every integral generalized sun graph with
at most $41$ vertices that is not a cycle has a cycle of length $4$ and is one
of the three graphs found by Braga, Rodrigues and Trevisan in 2017. Up to $42$
vertices, spectral lemmas exclude every cycle length $b\not\equiv0\pmod 4$
except $3$, $6$ and $10$. The lengths $3$ and $6$ are settled by two
classifications, and the length $10$ inside the proof of the main theorem;
for these three cases we give no written proof, and they rest on the formal
proofs. We also note that if an integral unicyclic graph has a cycle of odd
length $b$, then every prime $q$ with $(q-1)\mid(b-1)$ divides $2b$; hence
$3\mid b$, and $b=3$ or $b\ge15$ (after version~1 of this note, Braga, Moraes
and Santos proved that only $C_3$ occurs). Every result of the paper is formally
verified in Lean~4. The results were found and formalised by AI coding agents
working under the author's direction; their role is described at the end of
the paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A graph is \emph{integral} if all eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix are
integers, a notion that goes back to Harary and Schwenk~\cite{hs}. A search of
Braga, Rodrigues and Trevisan~\cite{brt} showed that, besides the cycles $C_3$,
$C_4$ and $C_6$, exactly three unicyclic graphs with at most $21$ vertices are
integral (the three graphs of Theorem~2 below; this classification is also
verified in Lean, Section~\ref{sec:lean}). Braga, Del-Vecchio and
Rodrigues~\cite{bdr} constructed infinite families of integral unicyclic
graphs by attaching pendant paths to the vertices of a cycle; such graphs are
called \emph{generalized sun graphs}.
Following~\cite{bms}, $C_b(n_1P_{t_1},\dots,n_bP_{t_b})$ is obtained from the
cycle $v_1\cdots v_bv_1$ by attaching $n_k$ copies of the path $P_{t_k}$ on
$t_k$ vertices to $v_k$ (joining $v_k$ to an end of each copy), and
$C_{b,t}(n_1,\dots,n_b)$ is the case $t_1=\dots=t_b=t$. We state the second
conjecture of~\cite{bdr} in our words.

\begin{conjtwo}[{\cite{bdr}; see~\cite[Conjecture~2]{bms}}]
Let $G$ be an integral generalized sun graph that is not a cycle. Then the
cycle of $G$ has length divisible by $4$.
\end{conjtwo}

Braga, Moraes and Santos~\cite{bms} proved the first conjecture of~\cite{bdr}
in a strong form: a graph with a pendant path of at least three edges has an
eigenvalue in $(1,2\cos(\pi/9)]$ (and one in $[-2\cos(\pi/9),-1)$), so it is
not integral~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bms}.
They disproved Conjecture~2: $C_{6,1}(0,6,6,12,6,6)$ has $42$ vertices and
spectrum $\{0^{[32]},\pm2^{[2]},\pm3^{[2]},\pm4\}$~\cite[Proposition~3.2]{bms}.
It is the smallest integral member, with $a=6$, of the family
$C_{6,1}(0,a,a,2a,a,a)$, $a\ge1$, which contains counterexamples with
arbitrarily many vertices~\cite[Corollary~3.6 and the remark after it]{bms}.
Their search over boxes of parameter vectors, for graphs with pendant vertices
only, found no other integral graph whose cycle length is not divisible by
$4$. As they point out~\cite[Section~4]{bms}, it ``does not prove that the
graph on 42 vertices has minimum order'' among all counterexamples, since
vectors outside the boxes may give smaller graphs, and graphs with copies of
$P_2$ were not searched. This note answers that question.

A \emph{counterexample} is a generalized sun graph that is integral, is not a
cycle, and has cycle length $b\not\equiv0\pmod4$. We allow any finite number of
pendant paths at each cycle vertex, possibly of different lengths at the same
vertex. By~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bms}, an integral generalized sun graph
then carries only copies of $P_1$ and $P_2$. The graphs
$C_b(n_1P_{t_1},\dots,n_bP_{t_b})$ form a subclass containing every graph
named below, so our results hold in either reading.

\begin{thmone}
Up to isomorphism, $C_{6,1}(0,6,6,12,6,6)$ is the only counterexample to
Conjecture~2 with at most $42$ vertices. In particular, the minimum order of a
counterexample is $42$.
\end{thmone}

\begin{thmtwo}
Every integral generalized sun graph with at most $41$ vertices that is not a
cycle has a cycle of length $4$, and is isomorphic to $C_{4,1}(6,0,3,0)$, with
$13$ vertices, or to $C_4(5P_2,P_1,2P_2,P_1)$ or $C_{4,2}(4,4,0,0)$, with $20$
vertices each.
\end{thmtwo}

These three graphs are the three integral unicyclic graphs found by Braga,
Rodrigues and Trevisan~\cite[Section~5, p.~296, and Table~1, p.~297]{brt}, with
spectra $\{0^{[9]},\pm2,\pm3\}$ and, for the two cospectral graphs,
$\{0^{[2]},\pm1^{[7]},\pm2,\pm3\}$; they are pairwise non-isomorphic.
Theorem~2 adds that there is no other integral generalized sun graph up to
$41$ vertices that is not a cycle. The one-page abstract of
Veras~\cite{ver} reports a computer search for integral unicyclic graphs. It
names $C_{4,1}(0,16,32,16)$, which has $68$ vertices and is integral, and the
family $C_{4,1}(0,a,2a,a)$, whose integral members with $a\ge1$ have at least
$68$ vertices~\cite{bms}. It does not state the range of the search and names no
graph with at most $41$ vertices, so nothing it reports overlaps Theorem~2,
although the search may have covered part of that range. Write $\mult(\lambda)$ for
the multiplicity of an eigenvalue $\lambda$. The proofs use the following
observation on integral graphs.

\begin{propthree}
Let $G$ be an integral graph with $e$ edges that contains an odd cycle, let $g$
be the length of its shortest odd cycles and $c_g$ their number. Then every
prime $q$ with $(q-1)\mid(g-1)$ divides $2g\,c_g$; in particular $3\mid g\,c_g$.
Moreover, at least $(g-1)/2$ integers $v\ge2$ satisfy $\mult(v)\ne\mult(-v)$,
so $2e\ge\sum_{v=2}^{(g+1)/2}v^2$.
\end{propthree}

For an integral unicyclic graph with a cycle of odd length $b$ this gives
$3\mid b$, and $b=3$ or $b\ge15$, with at least $102$ vertices in the latter
case (Corollary~\ref{cor:odd}).

\paragraph{Method.}
Up to $42$ vertices, hand proofs exclude odd $b\ge5$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:odd}), $b\in\{14,18\}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:1418}) and even
$b\ge22$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:cycle}), and a bound due to the first referee agent of
version~1 (see the disclosure; Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}) excludes every $b\ge14$
again. The cycle length $3$ is excluded by Proposition~\ref{prop:three}, the
cycle length $6$ is settled by Proposition~\ref{prop:six}, and the cycle length
$10$ is excluded inside the formal proof of Theorem~1
(Section~\ref{sec:proofs}). For these three cases we give no written proof.
Version~1 of this note settled them by exhaustive computer searches with
separately written programs; version~2 does not use these searches, and every
result stated here is proved in Lean~4 (Section~\ref{sec:lean}).
Section~\ref{sec:comp} keeps the mathematics behind the search rules of
version~1, as Lean-proved statements about formally defined sets of
sequences.

\section{Notation and spectral lemmas}\label{sec:lemmas}

Throughout, $G$ is a generalized sun graph with cycle $v_0v_1\cdots v_{b-1}v_0$
($b\ge3$, indices modulo $b$) carrying only copies of $P_1$ and $P_2$: at $v_k$
hang $p_k$ pendant vertices and $q_k$ pendant paths $v_kww'$. Put
$P=\sum p_k$, $Q=\sum q_k$, $K_p=\{k:p_k>0\}$, $K_q=\{k:q_k>0\}$,
$Z=\Z_b\setminus K_q$ and $A=A(G)$. Then $G$ has $n=b+P+2Q$ vertices and $n$
edges. Let $\Theta(G)=\sum_vd_v(d_v-1)$ over all vertices, with degrees $d_v$;
then $\Theta(G)=\sum_k\bigl((2+p_k+q_k)(1+p_k+q_k)+2q_k\bigr)$. An isomorphism
maps the unique cycle onto the unique cycle, and $(p_k,q_k)$ is determined by
the tree attached at $v_k$, so isomorphism classes correspond to dihedral
orbits of the sequences $((p_k,q_k))_{k\in\Z_b}$.

For a real symmetric matrix $M$, $n_+(M)$, $n_-(M)$ and $n_0(M)$ count its
positive, negative and zero eigenvalues. For a graph $H$, $N_{>t}(H)$,
$N_{\ge t}(H)$, $N_{<t}(H)$ and $N_{\le t}(H)$ count the eigenvalues of $A(H)$
in the indicated ranges, with multiplicity. If $G$ is integral, $B^+$ is the
multiset of its eigenvalues $\lambda\ge2$ and $S_2=\sum_{\lambda\in
B^+}\lambda^2$. For nonempty $K=\{k_1<\dots<k_t\}\subseteq\{0,\dots,b-1\}$ put
$k_{t+1}=k_1+b$; the \emph{gaps} of $K$ are the pairs $(k_i,k_{i+1})$, of
\emph{length} $k_{i+1}-k_i$. If $|K|=1$, the only gap has length $b$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:schur}
For $\lambda\in\R\setminus\{-1,0,1\}$ let
$f_k(\lambda)=p_k/\lambda+q_k\lambda/(\lambda^2-1)$ and
$S(\lambda)=A(C_b)-\lambda I+\diag(f_k(\lambda))_k$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] If $\lambda>1$, then $N_{>\lambda}(G)=n_+(S(\lambda))$ and
 $N_{\ge\lambda}(G)=n_+(S(\lambda))+n_0(S(\lambda))$.
\item[(b)] If $\lambda<-1$, then $N_{<\lambda}(G)=n_-(S(\lambda))$ and
 $N_{\le\lambda}(G)=n_-(S(\lambda))+n_0(S(\lambda))$.
\item[(c)] $N_{>1}(G)\ge|K_q|+n_+(R)$ and $N_{<-1}(G)\ge|K_q|+n_-(R')$, where
 $R$ and $R'$ are the principal submatrices of $A(C_b)-I+\diag(p_k)$ and
 $A(C_b)+I-\diag(p_k)$ on $Z$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With the cycle first,
$A-\lambda I=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A(C_b)-\lambda I&W\\W^{\mathsf
T}&H\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, where $H=H(\lambda)$ is the direct sum of a block
$(-\lambda)$ for each pendant vertex and a block
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-\lambda&1\\1&-\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ for
each pendant path (rows $w,w'$), and $W$ has one entry $1$ in the column of
each pendant vertex and each $w$, in the row of its neighbour on the cycle. $H$ has
eigenvalues $-\lambda$, $-\lambda\pm1$, so it is negative (positive) definite for
$\lambda>1$ ($\lambda<-1$). The $2\times2$ block has inverse with $(1,1)$ entry
$-\lambda/(\lambda^2-1)$, so $WH^{-1}W^{\mathsf T}=-\diag(f_k(\lambda))$ and
$A-\lambda I$ is congruent to $S(\lambda)\oplus H$; now use Sylvester's law of
inertia.

(c) As $\lambda\downarrow1$, the entries $f_k(\lambda)$, $k\in K_q$, tend to
$+\infty$ while the other entries of $S(\lambda)$ stay bounded; so the block
$S_{KK}$, $K=K_q$, is positive definite for $\lambda$ near $1$ and its inverse
tends to $0$. By Haynsworth's inertia additivity~\cite{hay},
$n_+(S(\lambda))=|K|+n_+(S_{ZZ}-S_{ZK}S_{KK}^{-1}S_{KZ})$, and the Schur
complement tends to $R$, so its positive inertia is eventually at least
$n_+(R)$. Finally $N_{>1}(G)=N_{>\lambda}(G)$ for $\lambda>1$ close to $1$. The
case $\lambda\uparrow-1$ is the same, with $f_k(\lambda)\to-\infty$ on $K_q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mult}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] Let $m\in\R\setminus\{-1,0,1\}$, $c_k=m-f_k(m)$,
 $T_k=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}c_k&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and
 $M=T_{b-1}\cdots T_0$. Then $\mult(m)=\dim\ker(M-I)\le2$; it equals $2$ if
 and only if $M=I$, and $1$ if and only if $M\ne I$ and $\tr M=2$.
\item[(b)] $\mult(0)=P-|K_p|+\delta_0$, where, if $K_p=\emptyset$, $\delta_0=2$
 when $4\mid b$ and $\delta_0=0$ otherwise, and, if $K_p\ne\emptyset$,
 $\delta_0$ is the number of gaps of $K_p$ of even length.
\item[(c)] For $\varepsilon=\pm1$, $\mult(\varepsilon)=Q-|K_q|+\delta_\varepsilon$,
 where $\delta_\varepsilon$ is the dimension of the space of $y\in\R^{\Z_b}$
 with $y_k=0$ for $k\in K_q$ and $y_{k-1}+y_{k+1}=\varepsilon(1-p_k)y_k$ for
 $k\notin K_q$. Each gap of $K_q$ contributes $0$ or $1$ to
 $\delta_\varepsilon$, and $\delta_\varepsilon\le2$ if $K_q=\emptyset$.
\item[(d)] $\mult(0)\le\max(P,2)$ and $\mult(\pm1)\le\max(Q,2)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) If $Ax=mx$, then $x_a=x_{v_k}/m$ at a pendant vertex $a$ of $v_k$, and
$x_{w'}=x_w/m$, $x_w=mx_{v_k}/(m^2-1)$ on a pendant path $v_kww'$. So $x$ is
determined by its restriction $y$ to the cycle, which satisfies
$y_{k-1}+y_{k+1}=c_ky_k$, and every solution $y$ extends to an eigenvector.
Writing $(y_{k+1},y_k)^{\mathsf T}=T_k(y_k,y_{k-1})^{\mathsf T}$, the solutions
correspond to the vectors fixed by $M$; as $\det M=1$, $M-I$ is singular if and
only if $\tr M=2$.

(b) If $Ax=0$, a pendant vertex at $v_k$ forces $x_{v_k}=0$, and a pendant
path gives $x_w=0$, $x_{w'}=-x_{v_k}$. For $k\in K_p$ the equation at $v_k$
only fixes the sum of the $p_k$ pendant values ($p_k-1$ free values), and for
$k\notin K_p$ it reads $y_{k-1}+y_{k+1}=0$. So $\delta_0$ is the dimension of
the space of $y$ with $y_k=0$ on $K_p$ and $y_{k-1}+y_{k+1}=0$ off $K_p$. If
$K_p=\emptyset$, then $y_{k+2}=-y_k$ for all $k$, with a $2$-dimensional space
of $b$-periodic solutions if $4\mid b$ and none otherwise. If
$K_p\ne\emptyset$, the equations split along the gaps. On a gap of length $d$
put $z_j=y_{k_i+j}$; then $z_0=z_d=0$ and $z_{j-1}+z_{j+1}=0$ for $0<j<d$,
which forces $z_j=0$ for even $j$ and $z_{j+2}=-z_j$ for odd $j$. For even $d$
this leaves $z_1$ free; for odd $d$ it gives $z_1=\pm z_d=0$.

(c) If $Ax=\varepsilon x$, a pendant path gives $x_{w'}=\varepsilon x_w$ and
$\varepsilon x_w=x_{v_k}+x_{w'}$, so $x_{v_k}=0$ for $k\in K_q$, and the equation
at $v_k$ only fixes the sum of the $q_k$ values $x_w$ ($q_k-1$ free values). A pendant vertex gives
$x_a=\varepsilon x_{v_k}$, so for $k\notin K_q$ the equation at $v_k$ reads
$y_{k-1}+y_{k+1}+\varepsilon p_ky_k=\varepsilon y_k$. On a gap of $K_q$ the
recurrence determines all values from the first one after the left end, so
the gap contributes at most $1$; if $K_q=\emptyset$ the periodic solutions of a
recurrence of order two form a space of dimension at most $2$.

(d) If $K_p\ne\emptyset$, then $\delta_0\le|K_p|$, the number of gaps, so
$\mult(0)\le P$; otherwise $\mult(0)=\delta_0\le2$. Likewise for $\pm1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:trace}
Let $H$ be a graph with $e$ edges, degrees $d_v$, $c_4$ cycles of length $4$,
and adjacency matrix $A$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] $\tr A^2=2e$ and $\tr A^4=2e+2\sum_vd_v(d_v-1)+8c_4$.
\item[(b)] If the shortest odd cycles of $H$ have length $g$ and there are
 $c_g$ of them, then $\tr A^L=0$ for odd $L<g$ and $\tr A^g=2g\,c_g$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We count closed walks. (a) A closed walk of length $4$ traverses one edge four
times ($2$ walks per edge), or two edges with a common vertex twice each ($4$
walks for each of the $\sum_v\binom{d_v}2$ such pairs), or a $4$-cycle ($8$ walks
per cycle). (b) A closed walk of odd length $L$ that repeats a vertex splits
there into two shorter closed walks, one of odd length. By induction it
contains an odd cycle of length at most $L$, with equality only if it
traverses a cycle once. So no closed walk of odd length $L<g$ exists, and each
$g$-cycle gives $2g$ closed walks of length $g$.
\end{proof}

For our graphs $e=n$, $c_4=[b=4]$, $\sum_vd_v(d_v-1)=\Theta(G)$, and $g=b$,
$c_g=1$ if $b$ is odd. If $G$ is integral, the eigenvalues in $\{0,\pm1\}$
satisfy $\lambda^4=\lambda^2$ and $\lambda^L=\lambda$ for odd $L$, and
$\tr A=0$, so
\begin{gather}
 \textstyle\sum_\lambda\lambda^2=2n,\qquad
 \sum_{|\lambda|\ge2}\lambda^2(\lambda^2-1)=2\,\Theta(G)+8\,[b=4],\label{eq:m4}\\
 \textstyle\sum_{|\lambda|\ge2}(\lambda^L-\lambda)=0\ \ (L\text{ odd},\ 3\le L<b),
 \qquad\sum_{|\lambda|\ge2}(\lambda^b-\lambda)=2b\ \ \text{if $b$ is odd}.\label{eq:odd}
\end{gather}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:adm}
Let $G$ be integral and not a cycle.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] The largest eigenvalue $\rho$ is simple and $\rho\ge3$; every
 eigenvalue satisfies $|\lambda|\le\rho$ and $|\lambda|\le\sqrt{2n}$, and
 $\lambda>-\rho$ if $b$ is odd.
\item[(b)] $\mult(\lambda)\le2$ whenever $|\lambda|\ge2$.
\item[(c)] If $b$ is even, the spectrum is symmetric, $S_2=n-\mult(1)$ and
 $P+Q\le S_2-b+2$. If $b$ is odd, $P+Q\le\frac12\sum_{|\lambda|\ge2}\lambda^2-b+2$.
\item[(d)] If $b$ is even, then $|B^+|\le4$ if $n\le41$, and $|B^+|\le5$ if
 $n\le42$, with equality only if $B^+=\{4,3,3,2,2\}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) By Perron--Frobenius theory~\cite{bh}, $\rho$ is simple, $|\lambda|\le\rho$,
and $-\rho$ is an eigenvalue only if $G$ is bipartite. As $G$ properly contains
$C_b$, $\rho>2$. Also $\lambda^2\le\tr A^2=2n$. (b) is
Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(a). (c) For even $b$, $G$ is bipartite and~\eqref{eq:m4}
gives $2n=2S_2+2\mult(1)$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(d),
$S_2\ge b+P+2Q-\max(Q,2)\ge b+P+Q-2$. For odd $b$,
$\sum_{|\lambda|\ge2}\lambda^2=2n-\mult(1)-\mult(-1)$, and the same estimate
applies. (d) By (a) and (b), $B^+$ has a simple largest element and contains
every other value at most twice. Five elements need $\rho\ge4$ and
$S_2\ge4^2+2\cdot3^2+2\cdot2^2=42$, with equality only for $\{4,3,3,2,2\}$, and
six need $S_2\ge5^2+4^2+2\cdot3^2+2\cdot2^2=67$; and $S_2\le n$ by (c).
\end{proof}

\section{Odd cycles}\label{sec:odd}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~3]
For $v\ge2$ let $\eta_v=\mult(v)-\mult(-v)$. For odd $L$ the eigenvalues in
$\{0,\pm1\}$ satisfy $\lambda^L=\lambda$, and $(-v)^L-(-v)=-(v^L-v)$, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:trace}(b) and $\tr A=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
 \sum_{v\ge2}\eta_v\,(v^L-v)=\tr A^L=0\ \ (L\text{ odd},\ L<g),\qquad
 \sum_{v\ge2}\eta_v\,(v^g-v)=2g\,c_g.
\end{equation}
Let $q$ be prime with $(q-1)\mid(g-1)$. For every integer $\lambda$,
$\lambda^g\equiv\lambda\pmod q$: this is clear if $q\mid\lambda$, and otherwise
$\lambda^{q-1}\equiv1$ by Fermat's little theorem, so
$\lambda^g=\lambda(\lambda^{q-1})^{(g-1)/(q-1)}\equiv\lambda$. Hence $q$ divides
$\sum_\lambda(\lambda^g-\lambda)=2g\,c_g$. The prime $q=3$ qualifies as $g$ is
odd.

Let $V=\{v\ge2:\eta_v\ne0\}$, $s=|V|$, and suppose $s\le(g-3)/2$. The
exponents $L=2j+1$, $1\le j\le s$, are odd and less than $g$, so
$\sum_{v\in V}\eta_vv(w_v^j-1)=0$ for $1\le j\le s$, with $w_v=v^2$. Subtracting
the row of the value $1$ from the other rows of the Vandermonde matrix of the
distinct numbers $1$ and $w_v$ ($v\in V$) shows that $\det(w_v^j-1)$ equals
that Vandermonde determinant, which is nonzero. Hence all $\eta_v=0$, which
contradicts the definition of $V$ if $s>0$ and~\eqref{eq:eta} if $s=0$. So
$s\ge(g-1)/2$, and $2e=\sum_\lambda\lambda^2\ge\sum_{v\in V}v^2\ge
\sum_{v=2}^{(g+1)/2}v^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:odd}
Let $G$ be an integral unicyclic graph with $n$ vertices whose cycle has odd
length $b$, and let $\kappa_b$ be the product of the primes $q$ with
$(q-1)\mid(b-1)$. Then $\kappa_b\mid2b$; in particular $3\mid b$. The odd
$b<200$ with $\kappa_b\mid2b$ are $3$, $15$, $27$, $39$, $63$, $75$, $87$,
$99$, $123$, $135$, $147$, $159$, $183$ and $195$. If $b\ne3$, then $b\ge15$ and
$n\ge102$.
\end{corollary}

After version~1 was released, Braga, Moraes and Santos~\cite{bms2} proved
that $C_3$ is the only integral unicyclic graph with a cycle of odd length,
which supersedes Corollary~\ref{cor:odd}; we keep it with its proof, which is
formalized.

\begin{proof}
Here $g=b$, $c_g=1$ and $e=n$, so $\kappa_b\mid2b$ by Proposition~3. For
$b=5,7,9,11,13$, $\kappa_b=30,42,30,66,2730$ does not divide $2b$; the list is a
direct computation. If $b\ge15$, then $2n\ge\sum_{v=2}^8v^2=203$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:novelty}
By the theorem of von Staudt and Clausen, $\kappa_b$ is the denominator of the
Bernoulli number $B_{b-1}$ for odd $b\ge3$. The first part of
Proposition~3 uses integrality; for an arbitrary integer matrix $A$ and a
prime $q$ one has the classical congruence $\tr A^q\equiv\tr A\pmod q$. The
second part is a variant of the odd-polynomial argument of van Dam and
Haemers~\cite[Section~2]{vdh}. Version~1 reported that searches on
26 September 2026 found no earlier statement of Proposition~3, even for
unicyclic graphs, but that the paper of Omidi~\cite{om} on necessary
conditions for integral unicyclic graphs and~\cite{bdr} could not be read in
full. As the argument is elementary, we present it as an observation.
\end{remark}

\section{Restricting the cycle length}\label{sec:cycle}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cycle}
$N_{>1}(G)\ge N_{>1}(C_b)=2\lceil b/6\rceil-1$ and
$N_{<-1}(G)\ge N_{<-1}(C_b)$. So if $G$ is integral and not a cycle, then
$|B^+|\ge2\lceil b/6\rceil-1$; if moreover $b$ is even, then $b\le12$ when
$n\le41$, and $b\le18$ when $n\le42$, with $B^+=\{4,3,3,2,2\}$ for
$b\in\{14,16,18\}$. In particular, if $n\le42$ and $b\equiv2\pmod 4$, then
$b\in\{6,10,14,18\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$C_b$ is an induced subgraph of $G$, so by Cauchy interlacing~\cite{bh} the
$i$-th largest (smallest) eigenvalue of $G$ is at least (at most) that of $C_b$
for $i\le b$. The eigenvalues of $C_b$ are $2\cos(2\pi j/b)$, $j\in\Z_b$, and
$2\cos(2\pi j/b)>1$ if and only if $6|j|<b$ for $j\in(-b/2,b/2]$, which holds
for $2\lceil b/6\rceil-1$ values of $j$. If $G$ is integral, its eigenvalues
greater than $1$ form $B^+$. Now apply Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}(d);
$2\lceil b/6\rceil-1=5$ for $13\le b\le18$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:count}
Let $G$ be integral with $b$ even, and $s=|B^+|$. Then
$\mult(\pm1)=n-S_2$ and $\mult(0)=2S_2-n-2s$. Hence $S_2\le n\le2S_2-2s$ and
$2S_2-n-2s\le\max(P,2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By symmetry $n=\mult(0)+2\mult(1)+2s$, and~\eqref{eq:m4} gives
$2n=2S_2+2\mult(1)$. The last inequality is Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(d).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:1418}
No integral generalized sun graph with cycle length $14$ or $18$ has at most
$42$ vertices.
\end{corollary}


\begin{proof}
The cycles $C_{14}$ and $C_{18}$ are not integral, as $2\cos(2\pi/b)$ is not an
integer for these $b$. Otherwise, by~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bms} the graph carries
only copies of $P_1$ and $P_2$, so it is a graph $G$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:lemmas} (see the remark before the proof of Theorem~1). By Lemma~\ref{lem:cycle}, $s=5$ and $S_2=42$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:count}, $42\le n\le42$, so $\mult(1)=0$ and
$\mult(0)=84-42-10=32\le\max(P,2)$. Then $n\ge b+P\ge46$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The next bound is due to the first referee agent of version~1 (see the
disclosure); it was the pruning rule of implementation~C of version~1
(Section~\ref{sec:comp}). For $t\ge1$ let
$\sigma(t)$ be the sum of the first $t$ terms of $4,4,4,4,9,9,9,9,16,\dots$, in
which each $v^2$, $v\ge2$, appears four times, and let $\sigma(t)=0$ for
$t\le0$. Thus $\sigma(9)=68$, $\sigma(10)=84$, $\sigma(11)=100$, $\sigma(14)=166$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rank}
Let $G$ be integral and not a cycle, $r$ the number of eigenvalues with
$|\lambda|\ge2$, and $r_0=b+|K_p|-\delta_0$, with $\delta_0,\delta_{\pm1}$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}. Then
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] $r=r_0+2|K_q|-\delta_1-\delta_{-1}\ge b-4$;
\item[(b)] $\sigma(r)\le2n$;
\item[(c)] $\sigma(r_0)\le2n-2|K_q|$ if $K_q\ne\emptyset$, and
 $\sigma(r_0-4)\le2n-4$ if $K_q=\emptyset$.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, $b\le13$ if $n\le42$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Since $r=n-\mult(0)-\mult(1)-\mult(-1)$, the formula follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(b),(c). If $K_p\ne\emptyset$, then $\delta_0\le|K_p|$ and
$r_0\ge b$; otherwise $K_q\ne\emptyset$ and $r_0\ge b-2$. With
$\delta_{\pm1}\le|K_q|$ if $K_q\ne\emptyset$ and $\delta_{\pm1}\le2$ otherwise,
$r\ge b-4$. (b) Each value $\pm v$, $v\ge2$, occurs at most twice
(Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}(b)), so the squares of these $r$ eigenvalues sum to at
least $\sigma(r)$ and at most $2n$. (c) If $K_q\ne\emptyset$, put
$x=2|K_q|-\delta_1-\delta_{-1}\ge0$; then $r=r_0+x$ and
$\mult(1)+\mult(-1)=2Q-x$, so
$\sigma(r_0)+4x\le\sigma(r)\le2n-2Q+x$, whence $\sigma(r_0)\le2n-2|K_q|$. If
$K_q=\emptyset$, put $y=\delta_1+\delta_{-1}\le4$; then $r=r_0-y$ and
$\sigma(r_0-4)+4(4-y)\le\sigma(r_0-y)\le2n-y$ (trivially if $r_0<4$), whence
$\sigma(r_0-4)\le2n-4$.

Let $n\le42$. If $b\ge15$, (a) and (b) give $100=\sigma(11)\le84$. If $b=14$,
then $r_0\ge14$ (as $4\nmid14$), and (c) gives $166\le82$ or $84\le80$.
\end{proof}

\section{The search rules of version 1}\label{sec:comp}
Version~1 settled the cycle lengths $3$, $6$ and $10$, and confirmed the other
cases, by exhaustive computer searches with four separately written programs,
called A, B (with a variant B2), C and D there; C and D were written by referee
agents. As described in version~1, each program ran through the sequences $((p_k,q_k))_{k\in\Z_b}$ with
$n\le N$, discarded sequences by spectral necessary conditions, and tested the
remaining graphs exactly for integrality. Version~2 does not use these
searches, and we make no claim about the programs or their runs; the programs,
logs and referee reports remain in the directory
\texttt{certificates/sun-graphs} of version~1.6.0 of~\cite{repo}. This section
keeps the mathematics behind their rules: the bounds used for discarding
partial assignments (Section~\ref{sec:prune}), the exact tests for
integrality (Section~\ref{sec:tests}), and properties of the sets of
sequences that the rules define, stated for formal definitions of these sets
(Section~\ref{sec:sets}). Everything stated in this section is proved in Lean.

\subsection{Soundness of the pruning}\label{sec:prune}
Let $X=\{0,\dots,b-2\}$. Below, a tridiagonal matrix indexed by $Y\subseteq X$
has off-diagonal entry $1$ in the positions $(k,k+1)$ and $(k+1,k)$ for which
both $k$ and $k+1$ lie in $Y$, and $0$ elsewhere.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prune}
Let $m\ge2$ and let $T$ be tridiagonal on $X$ with diagonal entries
$t_k\le f_k(m)-m$. Then $N_{>m}(G),N_{<-m}(G)\ge n_+(T)$ and
$N_{\ge m}(G),N_{\le-m}(G)\ge n_+(T)+n_0(T)$. If $Y\subseteq K_q\cap X$ and $T'$
is tridiagonal on $X\setminus Y$ with diagonal entries $t'_k\le p_k-1$, then
$N_{>1}(G),N_{<-1}(G)\ge|Y|+n_+(T')$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The principal submatrix $S(m)_X$ is tridiagonal, as $b-1\notin X$, and
$S(m)_X-T$ is diagonal and nonnegative. By Weyl's inequalities and Cauchy
interlacing~\cite{bh}, $n_+(S(m))\ge n_+(S(m)_X)\ge n_+(T)$, and likewise for
$n_++n_0$; apply Lemma~\ref{lem:schur}(a). As $f_k$ is odd, $\Delta
S(-m)_X\Delta=-S(m)_X$ for $\Delta=\diag((-1)^k)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:schur}(b)
gives the negative side. For $T'$, let $U=(K_q\cap X)\setminus Y$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:schur}(c) and interlacing, $N_{>1}(G)\ge|K_q|+n_+(R_{Z\cap X})$;
and $R_{Z\cap X}$ arises from the tridiagonal matrix on $X\setminus Y$ with
diagonal $p_k-1$ by deleting the indices in $U$, so
$n_+(R_{Z\cap X})\ge n_+(T')-|U|$, while $|K_q|\ge|Y|+|U|$. The negative side
is the same with $R'$ and $\Delta$.
\end{proof}


The node test of A (in its formal model, Section~\ref{sec:sets}) applies these
bounds to partial assignments. With $v_0,\dots,v_{d-1}$ assigned ($d\le b-1$), $T$ and $T'$ use the assigned
values and, elsewhere, those of a bare vertex ($t_k=-m$, $t'_k=-1$), with
$Y=K_q\cap\{0,\dots,d-1\}$. Raising $p_k$ or $q_k$ raises $t_k,t'_k$ or moves
$k$ into $Y$, which does not decrease $|Y|+n_+(T')$; so the bounds are
monotone (Proposition~\ref{prop:pruning}(d)). According to version~1, A
computed the inertias by the pivot recurrence $d_1=t_1$,
$d_k=t_k-1/d_{k-1}$, restarted after each index missing from the index set (a
tridiagonal matrix on an index set with gaps is block diagonal); the next
lemma shows that this recurrence gives the inertia.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pivot}
Let $T$ be symmetric tridiagonal with nonzero off-diagonal entries. If an exact
zero pivot $d_k=0$ is counted as negative, $d_{k+1}$ as positive, and the
recurrence is restarted with $d_{k+2}=t_{k+2}$, then the number of positive
pivots is $n_+(T)$; with the signs exchanged one obtains $n_-(T)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For small $\varepsilon>0$, $n_+(T)=n_+(T-\varepsilon I)$, whose pivots are
$\Delta_k(\varepsilon)/\Delta_{k-1}(\varepsilon)$ with leading minors $\Delta_k$.
The leading blocks are Jacobi matrices, with simple and strictly interlacing
eigenvalues; if $\Delta_k(0)=0$, counting their negative eigenvalues shows
that $\Delta_k(\varepsilon)$ and $\Delta_{k-1}(0)$ have opposite signs. The
recurrence $\Delta_{j+1}=t_{j+1}\Delta_j-a_j^2\Delta_{j-1}$ ($a_j\ne0$ the
off-diagonal entries) gives $\Delta_{k+1}(0)=-a_k^2\Delta_{k-1}(0)$ and
$\Delta_{k+2}(0)=t_{k+2}\Delta_{k+1}(0)$. So $d_k(\varepsilon)\to0^-$,
$d_{k+1}(\varepsilon)\to+\infty$, $d_{k+2}(\varepsilon)\to t_{k+2}$. For $n_-$
use $T+\varepsilon I$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:window}
If $v_0,\dots,v_{d-1}$ are assigned, contributing $\Theta_d$ to $\Theta(G)$ and
$W$ to $n-b$, and $r=b-d$, then every completion with $n\le N$ has
$\Theta_d+2r\le\Theta(G)\le\Theta_d+g(N-b-W)+2(r-1)$, where $g(w)=(2+w)(1+w)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A vertex of weight $w=p+2q$ contributes $g(w-q)+2q\le g(w)$, as
$g(t)-g(t-1)=2t+2$, and at least $g(0)=2$. As $g$ is convex,
$g(a)+g(c)\le g(a+c)+g(0)$, and $g$ is increasing.
\end{proof}

A partial assignment is discarded by this test if no admissible value of
$\Theta(G)$ from~\eqref{eq:m4} lies in the window. The formal model of version~6 of A also uses the following
consequence of Lemma~\ref{lem:count}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:partial}
Let $b$ be even, $G$ integral with $n\le N$, $s=|B^+|$ and
$n_{\max}=\min(N,2S_2-2s)$. Let $d\le b$, and let $P_d$ and $Q_d$ be the sums
of $p_k$ and $q_k$ over $k<d$. If $P_d\ge2$, then
$2Q_d\le2n_{\max}-b-2S_2+2s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:count}, $n\le2S_2-2s$, so $n\le n_{\max}$, and
$2S_2-n-2s\le\max(P,2)=P$, as $P\ge P_d\ge2$. Hence
$n=b+P+2Q\ge b+2S_2-n-2s+2Q$, that is, $2Q\le2n-b-2S_2+2s$, and $Q_d\le Q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact tests}\label{sec:tests}
Version~1 describes exact tests for integrality at the leaves of the searches;
they rest on the following facts. For a prime $\ell$ and a
finite set $S\subset\Z$ whose elements have distinct residues modulo $\ell$, and
a monic $f\in\Z[x]$, let $\mu_\ell(S,f)$ be the sum over $s\in S$ of the
multiplicity of the root $s$ of $f$ modulo $\ell$. Put
\[
 F(x)=\det\bigl(x(x^2-1)(xI-A(C_b))-\diag(p_k(x^2-1)+q_kx^2)\bigr).
\]
For an integer $m$ let $\beta_\ell(m)$ be the nullity modulo $\ell$ of the
following integer $b\times b$ matrix, plus an offset: for $m\notin\{0,\pm1\}$
the matrix $m(m^2-1)S(m)=m(m^2-1)(A(C_b)-mI)+\diag(p_k(m^2-1)+q_km^2)$ with no
offset; for $m=0$ the matrix of the system of Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(b) ($y_k=0$
for $k\in K_p$, $y_{k-1}+y_{k+1}=0$ otherwise) with the offset $P-|K_p|$; for
$m=\varepsilon=\pm1$ that of the system of Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(c) with the
offset $Q-|K_q|$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tests}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] For every finite graph $H$, $A(H)$ has $|V(H)|$ real eigenvalues
 counted with multiplicity; $\mult(m)$ is the nullity over $\Q$ of $A(H)-mI$
 for every integer $m$; $H$ is integral if and only if $\det(xI-A(H))$ is a
 product of factors $x-z$, $z\in\Z$; and if $H$ is integral with all
 eigenvalues in a finite set $S\subset\Z$, then $\sum_{m\in S}\mult(m)=|V(H)|$.
\item[(b)] If $n\le49$, every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $G$ satisfies
 $\lambda^2\le2n$ and $|\lambda|<10$. If moreover $G$ is integral, then
 $\det(xI-A)$ and $F$ are products of factors $x-z$ with $z\in[-9,9]$, and
 $\sum_{m=-9}^{9}\mult(m)=n$.
\item[(c)] $x^P(x^2-1)^QF(x)=x^b(x^2-1)^b\det(xI-A)$, and $F$ is monic of degree
 $4b$.
\item[(d)] $\mult(m)$ is the nullity over $\Q$ of $m(m^2-1)S(m)$ for every
 integer $m\notin\{0,\pm1\}$, and $\mult(0)$ and $\mult(\pm1)$ are the
 nullities over $\Q$ of the systems of Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(b),(c) plus the
 offsets $P-|K_p|$ and $Q-|K_q|$. For every integer square matrix and every
 prime $\ell$, the rank modulo $\ell$ is at most the rank over $\Q$, and ranks
 and nullities over $\Q$ and over $\R$ agree. Hence
 $\mult(m)\le\beta_\ell(m)$ for every prime $\ell$ and every integer $m$.
\item[(e)] Let $\ell$ be prime, $f\in\Z[x]$ monic and $S\subset\Z$ finite with
 distinct residues modulo $\ell$. Then $f$ modulo $\ell$ is monic of the same
 degree, the multiplicity of a root $z\in\Z$ of $f$ does not drop modulo
 $\ell$, and if $f$ is a product of factors $x-s$ with $s\in S$, then
 $\mu_\ell(S,f)=\deg f$. If $\ell>2R$, the integers in $[-R,R]$ have distinct
 residues modulo $\ell$.
\item[(f)] The numbers $10^9+7$, $998244353$, $2^{61}-1$, $10^{18}+3$ and
 $2^{31}-1$ are primes greater than $216$. Let $n\le49$ and let $G$ be
 integral. Then $\mu_\ell([-9,9],F)=4b$ for $\ell\in\{10^9+7,998244353,
 2^{61}-1\}$, $\mu_\ell([-10,10],\det(xI-A))=n$ for $\ell\in\{2^{61}-1,
 10^{18}+3\}$, and $n\le\sum_{m=-9}^{9}\beta_\ell(m)$ for $\ell=2^{31}-1$. So if
 one of these equalities or this inequality fails, $G$ is not integral.
\item[(g)] For a prime $\ell$ and $d<\ell$, the nodes $20,21,\dots,20+d$ are
 distinct modulo $\ell$, and Newton's divided differences on these nodes
 recover every polynomial of degree at most $d$ over $\Z/\ell$ from its values.
 For $\ell\in\{10^9+7,998244353,2^{61}-1\}$ and $3\le b\le49$, the
 determinants of the matrix defining $F$, evaluated modulo $\ell$ at these
 nodes, are the values of $F$ modulo $\ell$, and the reconstruction of degree
 $4b$ is $F$ modulo $\ell$.
\item[(h)] For every finite unicyclic graph $H$ and every edge $e=uv$ on its
 cycle $C$, $\det(xI-A(H))=\phi(H-e)-\phi(H-u-v)-2\phi(H-V(C))$, where
 $\phi(K)=\det(xI-A(K))$~\cite{sch}. For $G$ and $e=v_0v_{b-1}$,
 $\phi(G-V(C_b))=\prod_kx^{p_k}(x^2-1)^{q_k}$, and $\phi(G-e)$ and
 $\phi(G-v_0-v_{b-1})$ are given by an explicit two-term recurrence over the
 pairs $(p_k,q_k)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

The identity (c) follows from the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:schur}. According
to version~1, B computed $F$ modulo the three primes of (f) by interpolation, as
in (g), C computed $\det(xI-A)$ modulo its two primes by the formula (h), and D
computed the bounds $\beta_\ell(m)$ for $\ell=2^{31}-1$; each discarded a graph
when the corresponding test of (f) failed. Passing these tests does not imply
integrality, and nothing here says that it does.

According to version~1, implementation~A decided integrality with
Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(a), in integer arithmetic modulo four primes. For an integer $m$ with $|m|\ge2$ put
$h=m(m^2-1)$, $S_k=hc_k=m^2(m^2-1)-p_k(m^2-1)-q_km^2$ and
$\tilde T_k=hT_k=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}S_k&-h\\h&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
so that $\tilde M=\tilde T_{b-1}\cdots\tilde T_0=h^bM$, and let
$E=\prod_k(|S_k|+|h|)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(a), $\mult(m)=2$ if
$\tilde M=h^bI$, $\mult(m)=1$ if $\tilde M\ne h^bI$ and $\tr\tilde M=2h^b$, and
$\mult(m)=0$ otherwise.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:crt}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] The numbers $\ell_1=2^{62}-57$, $\ell_2=2^{62}-87$,
 $\ell_3=2^{62}-117$ and $\ell_4=2^{62}-143$ are pairwise distinct primes, and
 $\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4>2^{240}$. An integer $z$ with
 $|z|<\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4$ is $0$ if and only if $\ell_i\mid z$ for
 $i=1,\dots,4$.
\item[(b)] For all $b$, $p$, $q$ and $|m|\ge2$, $\tilde M$ is $h^b$ times the real
 matrix $M$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(a), every entry of $\tilde M$ has absolute
 value at most $E$, $|\tr\tilde M-2h^b|\le2E+2|h|^b$, and every entry of
 $\tilde M-h^bI$ has absolute value at most $E+|h|^b$.
\item[(c)] For each of the $23$ runs of A reported in version~1 (a version of A
 and a pair $(b,N)$), every sequence in the formal leaf set of that version at
 $(b,N)$ (Section~\ref{sec:sets}) and every $m$ with $2\le|m|\le9$, we have
 $2E+2|h|^b<2^{240}$. Hence $\tr\tilde M=2h^b$ if and only if
 $\tr\tilde M\equiv2h^b\pmod{\ell_i}$ for all $i$, $\tilde M=h^bI$ if and only if
 $\tilde M\equiv h^bI\pmod{\ell_i}$ for all $i$, and the multiplicity computed
 from these residues as above equals $\mult(m)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Formal search sets}\label{sec:sets}
The following definitions model the rules of the programs of version~1. They
are the definitions of the Lean statement files, which state them in full; we
make no claim that the programs implement them. A \emph{sequence} for $(b,N)$
is a pair $(p,q)$ of maps $\Z_b\to\mathbb N$ with $P+2Q>0$ and
$n=b+P+2Q\le N$, so that $G=G(p,q)$ is a graph of Section~\ref{sec:lemmas} that
is not a cycle.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{feature vector} is a pair $s=(s^+,s^-)$ of maps
$\{2,\dots,9\}\to\{0,1,2\}$, standing for $v\mapsto\mult(v)$ and
$v\mapsto\mult(-v)$. Put $\epsilon_b=0$ for even $b$ and $\epsilon_b=1$ for odd
$b$, $s_v=s^+(v)+\epsilon_bs^-(v)$, $S_2(s)=\sum_vs_vv^2$ and
$M_4(s)=\sum_vs_vv^2(v^2-1)$. The vector $s$ is \emph{admissible} for $(b,N)$
if some $\rho\ge3$ has $s^+(\rho)=1$ and $s^+(v)=0$ for $v>\rho$; $s^-=0$ if $b$
is even, and $s^-(v)=0$ for $v\ge\rho$ if $b$ is odd; $S_2(s)\le(1+\epsilon_b)N$;
and, if $b$ is odd, $\sum_v(s^+(v)-s^-(v))(v^L-v)$ is $0$ for odd $L$ with
$3\le L<b$ and $2b$ for $L=b$ (compare Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}
and~\eqref{eq:odd}). With the \emph{cycle filter}, also $\sum_vs^+(v)$ is at
least the number $2\lceil b/6\rceil-1$ of eigenvalues of $C_b$ greater than
$1$, and for odd $b$, $\sum_vs^-(v)$ is at least the number of eigenvalues of
$C_b$ less than $-1$ (compare Lemma~\ref{lem:cycle}).

A sequence passes the \emph{moment test} if some feature vector $s$,
admissible for $(b,N)$, has $M_4(s)=\Theta(G)+4[b=4]$ and
$S_2(s)\le n$ if $b$ is even, and $M_4(s)=2\Theta(G)$ and $S_2(s)\le2n$ if $b$
is odd; with the \emph{load bound}, also $n-\max(Q,2)\le S_2(s)$ (for odd $b$,
$2n-2\max(Q,2)\le S_2(s)$), compare~\eqref{eq:m4} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}(c).

$\mathrm{Cand}_B(b,N)$ is the set of sequences for $(b,N)$ that pass the moment
test with the load bound and without the cycle filter (the formal model of the
moment test of B, version~1, and, for even $b$, of D, version~2). Its subset
$\mathrm{Can}_B(b,N)$ consists of the sequences that are lexicographically
least among their images under the dihedral group of $\Z_b$, comparing the
keys $1000p_k+q_k$ for $k=0,1,\dots$ in turn, and $\mathrm{Can}_D(b,N)$ of
those that are greatest, for the keys $64(p_k+2q_k)+p_k$.

$\mathrm{Cand}_{B3}(b,N)$ (the formal model of version~3 of B) is the set of sequences that pass
the moment test with the cycle filter, the load bound and, for even $b$, the
inequalities $0\le2S_2(s)-n-2\sum_vs^+(v)\le\max(P,2)$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:count}; whose key $1000p_0+q_0$ is at least that of every
other vertex; and that pass, for every $d$ with $1\le d\le b$, the window of
Lemma~\ref{lem:window} with some feature vector $s$ admissible with the cycle
filter (for $d=b$ the upper end of the window is $\Theta_d$ itself; for even $b$, $s$ must also satisfy
$\max(b+P_d+2Q_d,S_2(s))\le n_{\max}$ and the inequality of
Lemma~\ref{lem:partial}, with $S_2(s)$ and $\sum_vs^+(v)$ in place of $S_2$ and
$s$). The leaf set of C at $(b,N)$, with the rank bound, consists of
the sequences with $\sigma\bigl(r_0-4[K_q=\emptyset]\bigr)\le2N$, in the
notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}, that are least among their dihedral images
for the keys $[p_k>0]+2[q_k>0]$.
\end{definition}

For each modelled version of each program (A, versions~4 to~6; B, versions~1
to~3; C and C2; D, versions~1 to~3), the statement files define in the same
way the leaf set at $(b,N)$, from the node test of A (Lemma~\ref{lem:prune}
with the bounds described after it, Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}(c) and~\eqref{eq:m4},
and optionally Lemma~\ref{lem:partial}), the window of
Lemma~\ref{lem:window}, the rotation rules, canonical forms and moment tests
of B and D, and the rules of C. D's pruning is modelled by recursively defined
tables $\mathrm{reach}_1(r,w,t)$ and $\mathrm{reach}_2(r,w,Q,t)$ for $t<3072$
and a predicate $\mathrm{Reach}_D$ on partial assignments, which is true if
some multiset of eigenvalues of the required shape is compatible with some
completion.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pruning}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] Every admissible feature vector is dominated, in $M_4$, $S_2$ and
 the numbers of values greater than $m$ and at least $m$ for $1\le m\le9$, by
 one that is maximal for this order. (For odd $b$ these numbers are the
 smaller of the counts on the positive and on the negative side, and the
 number of values at least $m$ is taken to be $0$ for $m=1$.)
\item[(b)] For $b\le N$, the recursive enumeration of the statement files
 produces exactly the sequences for $(b,N)$.
\item[(c)] Let $3\le b\le N\le49$ and let $G=G(p,q)$ be integral, with $(p,q)$ a
 sequence for $(b,N)$. Then $s^+(v)=\mult(v)$ and, for odd $b$,
 $s^-(v)=\mult(-v)$ (for even $b$, $s^-=0$) define a feature vector that
 satisfies all conditions of admissibility except possibly the cycle filter.
\item[(d)] For $N\le49$, the node test of A is monotone: if it holds at depth
 $d<b$ for
 $(p',q')$ and $p\le p'$, $q\le q'$ componentwise, it holds for $(p,q)$.
\item[(e)] Let $3\le b$ and $N\le49$. If $(p,q)$ is a sequence for $(b,N)$ and
 $G$ is integral, then $(p,q)$ passes the window test with the cycle filter at
 every depth $d\le b$.
\item[(f)] For $t<3072$, $\mathrm{reach}_1(r,w,t)$ holds if and only if some
 pair of maps $\Z_r\to\mathbb N$ has weight $P+2Q=w$ and $\Theta$-sum $t$, and
 likewise $\mathrm{reach}_2$ with $Q$ prescribed; and, for even $b$ and
 $b\le N\le49$, $\mathrm{Reach}_D$ at a partial assignment of
 $v_0,\dots,v_{d-1}$ whose assigned weight is at most $N-b$ holds if and only if some completion with $n\le N$ passes
 the moment test.
\item[(g)] For every modelled version and every $(b,N)$ in its domain
 ($3\le b\le N\le49$, with bounds on $b$ for each version), every integral $G$
 with $(p,q)$ a sequence for $(b,N)$ has a dihedral image in the leaf set of
 that version.
\item[(h)] For every modelled version and $(b,N)$ in its domain, a sequence in
 the leaf set has a factorisation of $\det(xI-A)$ into
 factors $x-z$, $z\in\Z$, if and only if $G$ is integral; and, for a sequence
 $(p,q)$ for $(b,N)$, $G$ is integral if and only if some dihedral image of
 $(p,q)$ is such a leaf.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:empty}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] For odd $b$ with $5\le b\le15$ and $N\in\{41,42\}$, no feature
 vector is admissible for $(b,N)$.
\item[(b)] For $b\in\{22,26\}$ and $N=42$, no feature vector is admissible with

\item[(c)] $\mathrm{Cand}_{B3}(14,42)$ and $\mathrm{Cand}_{B3}(18,42)$ are
 empty, and so is the leaf set of C with the rank bound at $(13,41)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\section{Small cycle lengths and the proofs of Theorems 1 and 2}\label{sec:proofs}

The following three classifications were obtained in version~1 only as results
of the computer searches. We have no written proofs of them; they are proved
in Lean (Section~\ref{sec:lean}, where the routes of the formal proofs are
outlined).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:three}
Every integral generalized sun graph with cycle length $3$ that is not a cycle
has at least $50$ vertices.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:six}
The images of $(0,6,6,12,6,6)$ under the dihedral group of $\Z_6$ are its six
rotations, which are distinct. Let $b=6$ and let $p,q\colon\Z_6\to\mathbb N$,
not both zero, with $n\le42$. Then $G(p,q)$ is integral if and only if $q=0$
and $p$ is one of these six rotations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:four}
An integral generalized sun graph with cycle length $4$ that is not a cycle
and has at most $49$ vertices is isomorphic to $C_{4,1}(6,0,3,0)$,
$C_4(5P_2,P_1,2P_2,P_1)$, $C_{4,2}(4,4,0,0)$ or $C_{4,2}(10,10,0,0)$, and these
four graphs are integral. The last one has $44$ vertices, characteristic
polynomial $x^2(x^2-1)^{19}(x^2-9)(x^2-16)$ and spectrum
$\{0^{[2]},\pm1^{[19]},\pm3,\pm4\}$.
\end{proposition}

Let $H$ be a generalized sun graph with cycle length $b$ whose pendant paths
have at most two vertices. Reordering the paths at each cycle vertex shows that
$H$ is isomorphic to a graph $G(p,q)$ of Section~\ref{sec:lemmas}, and $H$ is
connected, with $n$ vertices and $n$ edges, so it is unicyclic and its cycle
has length $b$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~1]
Let $G$ be a counterexample with $n\le42$; by~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bms} it
carries only copies of $P_1$ and $P_2$, so we may assume $G=G(p,q)$. If
$b=3$, then $n\ge50$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:three}. Odd $b\ge5$ is excluded
by Corollary~\ref{cor:odd}, as it would give $n\ge102$. If
$b\equiv2\pmod4$, then $b\in\{6,10,14,18\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:cycle}, and
$b\notin\{14,18\}$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:1418}. If $b=6$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:six} gives $q=0$ and a rotation $p$ of
$(0,6,6,12,6,6)$; the rotation of the indices is an isomorphism
$G\cong C_{6,1}(0,6,6,12,6,6)$. This graph is a counterexample: it is integral
by~\cite[Proposition~3.2]{bms}, has $42$ vertices, and $4\nmid6$. The
remaining case $b=10$ is excluded only in the formal proof of Theorem~1; we
give no written proof of it. As isomorphic graphs have the same number of
vertices, every counterexample has at least $42$ vertices.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~2]
Let $G$ be integral, not a cycle, with $n\le41$ and cycle length $b$. If
$4\nmid b$, then $G$ is a counterexample with $n\le42$, so by Theorem~1 it is
isomorphic to $C_{6,1}(0,6,6,12,6,6)$, which has $42$ vertices; this is
impossible. So $4\mid b$, and by~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bms} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:cycle}, $b\le12$. If $b=4$, Proposition~\ref{prop:four} gives
one of four graphs, and the one with $44$ vertices is excluded. The remaining
cases $b=8$ and $b=12$ are excluded only in the formal proof of Theorem~2; we
give no written proof of them.
\end{proof}

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}
The questions of~\cite[Section~5]{bms}, whether the cycle length $b$ of an
integral generalized sun graph other than a cycle must be even, and whether $4$
and $6$ are the only possible values, are not answered here. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:odd}, an odd $b$ is $3$, or at least $15$ with $n\ge102$;
and $b=3$ needs $n\ge50$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:three}).
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} bounds $b$ for either parity: if $r_{\max}(n)$ is the
largest $t$ with $\sigma(t)\le2n$, then $b\le r_{\max}(n)+4$; writing
$r_{\max}(n)=4k+j$ with $0\le j\le3$, we have
$2n\ge\sigma(4k)\ge4\sum_{v=1}^kv^2\ge\frac43k^3$, so $b\le(96n)^{1/3}+7$.

for the question answered here.

\paragraph{Routes of the formal proofs.}
We outline the formal proofs only where the paper gives no written proof;
elsewhere the formal proofs may follow other routes than the written ones.
\emph{Theorem~1.} The formal proof reduces a counterexample with $n\le42$ to the
cycle lengths $6$ and $10$, settles $b=6$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:six}, and
excludes $b=10$, after showing by Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} that no pendant path $P_2$ occurs, by a case analysis of the multiplicities of the eigenvalues
$2,\dots,6$ allowed by the lemmas of Sections~\ref{sec:lemmas}
and~\ref{sec:cycle}, combined with congruences modulo~$4$ obtained from the
transfer matrices of Lemma~\ref{lem:mult}(a).
\emph{Theorem~2.} The formal proof excludes $b=8$ and $b=12$ by case analyses
of the possible spectra, using transfer-matrix identities, rank bounds and
parity arguments on the supports of $p$ and $q$.
\emph{Proposition~\ref{prop:three}.} The formal proof reduces the case $b=3$ to
three residual configurations, and for each shows, by a kernel-checked search over
the possible integer root patterns of a polynomial attached to the graph (a table
of candidate rows whose completeness is itself proved in Lean), that every pattern
is rejected by an explicit identity.
\emph{Propositions~\ref{prop:six} and~\ref{prop:four}.} After the reduction to
pendant paths with at most two vertices, the formal proofs exclude or
identify the graphs by case analyses of the possible spectra (for
Proposition~\ref{prop:six}, the largest eigenvalue $\rho\in\{3,4,5,6\}$) and
of the positions of the pendant vertices and paths; the integrality of the
graphs found is proved from their characteristic polynomials.
\emph{The classification of unicyclic graphs with at most $21$ vertices}
(Section~1) is proved for arbitrary unicyclic graphs: the formal proof shows
that in an integral one with at most $21$ vertices every vertex off the cycle
has degree at most~$2$, so that it is a generalized sun graph, and then uses
the results on generalized sun graphs.
\emph{Proposition~\ref{prop:counts}(a).} At $(6,42)$, Burnside's lemma for the
dihedral group of order $12$ reduces the sizes of $\mathrm{Can}_B$ and
$\mathrm{Can}_D$ to the size of $\mathrm{Cand}_B(6,42)$ and to the numbers of
sequences fixed by the reflections and rotations, which are proved separately.
The set $\mathrm{Cand}_B(6,42)$ is split into $21$ fibres by the value of
$\Theta(G)$; the ten fibres completed by Claude Code were split into slices by the value of $Q$, and the size of each
slice was computed by closed reduction in Lean's metaprogramming layer, and
the value is checked by \texttt{rfl} in the kernel.

No Lean check rejected a module in any of the three builds.

Lean bounds the work of each elaboration step by a number of ``heartbeats''.
In the $190$ slice modules and the module that assembles
\texttt{LegacyMomentCounts}, written by Claude Code, this limit is removed
(\texttt{set\_option maxHeartbeats 0}) for the computation of the counts and
their checks in the kernel (the values were found by reduction in the
metaprogramming layer and are only trusted after the kernel has re-derived them
by \texttt{rfl}). It is raised to $800{,}000$ in eight proof modules
written by Codex (prime certificates used for Propositions~\ref{prop:tests}(f)
and~\ref{prop:crt}(a)), and set explicitly, to $200{,}000$ or $1{,}000{,}000$,
in three test files of the frozen statements. The limit only bounds how long
Lean may work on a step; it does not change what is accepted. The soundness of
the proofs rests on the Lean kernel, which checks every proof term, as
\texttt{leanchecker} did again for every module.

\paragraph{Faithfulness and non-claims.}
In the formal statements a generalized sun graph is given by $b$ and, for each
cycle vertex, a list of the numbers of vertices of the paths attached to it;
the results stated for arbitrary generalized sun graphs (Theorems~1 and~2,
\cite[Theorem~2.1]{bms}, Corollary~\ref{cor:1418} and
Propositions~\ref{prop:three} and~\ref{prop:four}) are formalised in this
generality, and the others for the graphs $G(p,q)$. Corollary~\ref{cor:1418}
is formalised also for the cycles $C_{14}$ and $C_{18}$. The statements of
Section~\ref{sec:comp} concern formally defined sets and predicates modelled on
the rules of the programs of version~1; no statement says that the programs
implement them, that their runs were complete, or what they printed. B2, which
used floating-point arithmetic, is not modelled. Passing a test of
Proposition~\ref{prop:tests}(f) is not claimed to imply integrality. The $23$
runs of Proposition~\ref{prop:crt}(c) are a list of data taken from version~1.
The identifications of the families of Section~1 with those of~\cite{bms}
and~\cite{ver}, and of the graphs of Theorem~2 with those of~\cite{brt}, are
bibliographic and not formalised; so are Conjecture~2 as a conjecture of
\cite{bdr} and the questions of Section~\ref{sec:open}. That a generalized
sun graph whose paths have at most two vertices is unicyclic and isomorphic to
some $G(p,q)$, used in the written proofs of Section~\ref{sec:proofs}, is not
a separate formal statement; the formal proofs of the results that use it
prove what they need. The agreement of the Lean definitions with those of the
paper was checked by the reviewing agents, not by a machine.
