\begin{abstract}
The binary Reed--Muller code $\RM(7,14)$ has minimum distance $d=128$.
Leuenberger and Albrizzio (arXiv:2606.21425) showed that every even number from
$312$ to $2^{14}-312$ is a weight of $\RM(7,14)$, except possibly $22$ values.
We show that $14$ of them are weights, by explicit codewords. We also give
explicit codewords for all even numbers from $320$ to $2^{14}-320$ except $322$,
$326$, $330$, $334$ and their complements. Hence, for the middle-interval
formulation of an open question of Carlet used here, at $(c,m)=(7,14)$ every
even number in $[320,16064]$ is a weight if and only if $322$, $326$, $330$
and $334$ are weights. For them we prove non-existence in
restricted families and reduce each of them to a question about $\RM(6,13)$ and
$\RM(7,13)$. We also give a proof of a result of Lou and Wang: their
Conjecture~2, which would imply this middle-interval assertion, is false for
$m=6$. Every
result of the paper is formally verified in Lean~4. The results were found and
formalised by AI coding agents working under the author's direction; their role
is described at the end of the paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For $0\le r\le m$, the binary Reed--Muller code $\RM(r,m)$ consists of the
truth tables on $\F_2^m$ of the polynomials in $x_1,\dots,x_m$ over $\F_2$ of
degree at most $r$, where $x_i^2=x_i$. We identify a codeword with its
polynomial (its algebraic normal form), write $\wt f$ for the number of points
at which $f=1$, and call the set of weights of the codewords the \emph{weight
spectrum}. The minimum distance of $\RM(r,m)$ is $d=2^{m-r}$. Kasami and
Tokura~\cite{kt} determined the weights below $2d$, and Kasami, Tokura and
Azumi~\cite{kta} those below $2.5d$. The whole weight spectrum of $\RM(m-c,m)$
is known, for all $m\ge2c$, for $c\le2$ (classical), for $c=3,4$ (Carlet and
Sol\'e~\cite{cs}), for $c=5$ (Carlet~\cite{c24}) and for $c=6$ (Lou and
Wang~\cite{lw}).
Carlet asked~\cite{c24} whether, for every $c\ge1$ and $m\ge2c$, the weight
spectrum of $\RM(m-c,m)$ consists of $0$, $2^m$, the weights in $[2^c,2^{c+1}]$
given by Kasami and Tokura and in $[2^{c+1},2^{c+1}+2^{c-1}]$ given by Kasami,
Tokura and Azumi, their complements to $2^m$, and \emph{all} even numbers in
$[2^{c+1}+2^{c-1},2^m-2^{c+1}-2^{c-1}]$. (We paraphrase the wording of Carlet's
extended abstract~\cite{cdmd} of~\cite{c24}, which is also that
of~\cite[Conjecture~1]{la}; we could not access the full text of~\cite{c24}.
In the earlier conjecture of Carlet and Sol\'e~\cite[Section~6]{cs} the last
interval starts at $3\cdot2^c$.) Since $d=2^c$, the last interval starts at
$2.5d$. The first open case is $(c,m)=(7,14)$: here $d=128$, and the last
interval is $[320,2^{14}-320]=[320,16064]$.

Leuenberger and Albrizzio~\cite[Proposition~4]{la} showed that every even
number from $312$ to $2^{14}-312$ is a weight of $\RM(7,14)$, except possibly
the $22$ elements of
\begin{multline*}
M=\{322,326,330,334,354,8174,8178,8182,8186,8188,8190,\\
8194,8196,8198,8202,8206,8210,16030,16050,16054,16058,16062\};
\end{multline*}
parts of their proof rest on computer searches whose details are not given
(Lemmas~6 and~9 of~\cite{la}). They were unsure whether this is the whole
spectrum, and remarked that their constructions would produce all of $M$ from
the eight weights $322,326,330,334,354,4378,4380,4382$. Their constructions
follow Lou and Wang, whose Conjecture~2~\cite{lw} would give $322$, $326$,
$330$, $334$ and $354$ (see Theorem~C).

\paragraph{Results.}
Throughout, we write
\begin{gather*}
U_0=\{322,326,330,334\},\qquad U=U_0\cup(2^{14}-U_0)=U_0\cup\{16050,16054,16058,16062\},\\
K=\{0,128,192,224,240,248,252,254,256,272,288,296,304,308,312,314,316,318\}.
\end{gather*}
The set $K$ consists of the numbers below $320$ that~\cite[Proposition~4]{la}
asserts to be weights of $\RM(7,14)$. Its elements are weights (Theorem~B).
Version~1 of this paper also asserted, citing Kasami, Tokura and
Azumi~\cite{kta}, that they are all the weights below $320$; we could not
consult~\cite{kta}, and we make no claim about other numbers below $320$.

\begin{thmA}
The following $17$ numbers are weights of $\RM(7,14)$: $354$, $4378$, $4380$,
$4382$, $8174$, $8178$, $8182$, $8186$, $8188$, $8190$, $8194$, $8196$,
$8198$, $8202$, $8206$, $8210$ and $16030$. Table~\ref{tab:wit} gives
codewords with at most seven monomials.
\end{thmA}

Fourteen of these numbers, the elements of $M\setminus U$, are listed
in~\cite{la} as possibly missing; to our knowledge they are new. The other three, $4378$,
$4380$ and $4382$, are not new: they are weights by~\cite[Proposition~4]{la},
and the remark of~\cite{la} lists them among the eight weights from which its
constructions would produce $M$. We include codewords for them because we do
not use~\cite[Proposition~4]{la}.

\begin{thmB}
Let $S_0=\bigl(K\cup\{320,322,\dots,16064\}\cup(2^{14}-K)\bigr)\setminus U$.
Every element of $S_0$ is the weight of an explicit codeword of $\RM(7,14)$ with
at most eight monomials.
\end{thmB}

In Carlet's question the weights below $2.5d$ and their complements are, by
definition, those given by~\cite{kt,kta}, and all weights are even by (P1)
in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. So we read the question, for $(c,m)=(7,14)$, as
asking whether every even number from $320$ to $16064$ is a weight.

\begin{corollary}
For $(c,m)=(7,14)$, Carlet's question in this reading has a positive answer if
and only if $322$, $326$, $330$ and $334$ are weights of $\RM(7,14)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If every even number from $320$ to $16064$ is a weight, so are these four.
Conversely, if they are weights, so are their complements, by (P1); with
Theorem~B, every even number from $320$ to $16064$ is a weight, since those
not in $S_0$ are the elements of $U$.
\end{proof}

Construction~2 of~\cite{lw} takes
$g_1=x_1\cdots x_m+x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_m}+x_{i_{m+1}}\cdots x_{i_{2m}}$ and
$g_2=x_1\cdots x_m+x_{i_{2m+1}}\cdots x_{i_{3m}}+x_{i_{3m+1}}\cdots x_{i_{4m}}$
with $1\le i_j\le2m$, and $a_1,a_2\in\F_2$, and forms the concatenation
$g=0\cat(g_1+a_1)\cat g_2\cat(g_1+g_2+a_2)\in\RM(m+1,2m+2)$ of four truth
tables (Section~\ref{sec:lw}). Conjecture~2 of~\cite{lw} states that, for
$m\ge4$, the weights of these codewords include $2^{m+2}+2^m+2i$ and
$2^{2m}+2^m+2i$ for $0\le i<2^m$; for $m=6$ the first family is
$\{320,322,\dots,446\}$, which contains $322$, $326$, $330$, $334$ and $354$.

\begin{thmC}
Conjecture~2 of~\cite{lw} is false for $m=6$: no codeword of Construction~2
with $m=6$ has weight $322$.
\end{thmC}

No sum of at most three indicators of flats of dimension at least $7$

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
A \emph{$k$-flat} is an affine subspace of dimension $k$, with $2^k$ points; its
indicator $1_F$ is a product of $m-k$ affine functions, so it lies in
$\RM(m-k,m)$. A nonempty intersection of flats is a flat. We write
$x_S=\prod_{i\in S}x_i$ ($x_\emptyset=1$). Affine bijections preserve degrees,
weights and flats. We use the following known results.

{\setlength{\leftmargini}{3.2em}%
\begin{itemize}
\item[(P1)] If $r<m$, every codeword of $\RM(r,m)$ has even weight, since
$\wt f\bmod2$ is the coefficient of $x_1\cdots x_m$ in $f$. As
$\wt(f+1)=2^m-\wt f$, the weight spectrum is invariant under $w\mapsto2^m-w$.
\item[(P2)] For $r\ge1$, the weights of $\RM(r,m)$ are divisible by
$2^{\lfloor(m-1)/r\rfloor}$ (McEliece~\cite{mc}). In particular the weights of
$\RM(6,13)$ are divisible by $4$.
\item[(P3)] For $r\le m$, the minimum distance of $\RM(r,m)$ is $d=2^{m-r}$, and
the codewords of weight $d$ are the indicators of $(m-r)$-flats
\cite[Chapter~13]{ms}.
\end{itemize}}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ie}
For $S_1,\dots,S_k\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}$,
\[
\wt\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^kx_{S_i}\Bigr)=\sum_{\emptyset\ne T\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}}
(-2)^{|T|-1}\,2^{m-|\bigcup_{i\in T}S_i|}.
\]
In particular, the weight depends only on the sizes of the $2^k-1$ Venn regions
of $S_1,\dots,S_k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $y_1,\dots,y_k\in\{0,1\}$ the exclusive or of the $y_i$ equals
$\sum_{\emptyset\ne T}(-2)^{|T|-1}\prod_{i\in T}y_i$, by induction on $k$ from
$y\oplus y'=y+y'-2yy'$. Put $y_i=x_{S_i}(x)$ and sum over $x\in\F_2^m$: the
product over $T$ equals $1$ exactly when $x_j=1$ for all
$j\in\bigcup_{i\in T}S_i$, which happens at $2^{m-|\bigcup_{i\in T}S_i|}$
points. The size of a union is a sum of sizes of Venn regions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:halves}
Let $f\colon\F_2^{m+1}\to\F_2$, and put $g(x)=f(x,0)$ and $g'(x)=f(x,1)$ for
$x\in\F_2^m$. Then $f=g+x_{m+1}(g+g')$, and $f\in\RM(r+1,m+1)$ if and only if
$g\in\RM(r+1,m)$ and $g+g'\in\RM(r,m)$. Moreover
$\wt f=\wt g+\wt g'=\wt(g+g')+2|\supp g\cap\supp g'|$, and
$\supp g\cap\supp g'=\supp g\setminus\supp(g+g')$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The identity holds at $x_{m+1}=0$ and at $x_{m+1}=1$. The polynomials $g$ and
$x_{m+1}(g+g')$ have no monomial in common, so $\deg f\le r+1$ if and only if
$\deg g\le r+1$ and $\deg(g+g')\le r$. The rest is counting.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~A]
Each $f_w$ in Table~\ref{tab:wit} is a sum of distinct monomials of degree at
most $7$, so $f_w\in\RM(7,14)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ie}, $\wt
f_w=\sum_j(-2)^{j-1}\sigma_j$ with the $\sigma_j$ of the table; for instance
$\wt f_{354}=640-2\cdot203+4\cdot50-8\cdot12+16=354$. The other seven weights
are those of the complements $1+f_w$, by (P1); they are sums of at most seven
monomials as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:direct}
The weights $8174$ to $8210$ also follow from a direct sum: if $A$ depends only
on $x_1,\dots,x_7$ and $B$ only on $x_8,\dots,x_{14}$, then $\deg(A+B)\le7$ and
$\wt(A+B)=\wt A(128-\wt B)+(128-\wt A)\wt B=8192-2(64-\wt A)(64-\wt B)$. As
$\wt A$ and $\wt B$ can take any value from $0$ to $128$, taking $\wt B=63$ and
$\wt A=64-n$ shows that every even number $8192-2n$ with $|n|\le64$ is a
weight.
\end{remark}

\section{Construction 2 of Lou and Wang: Theorem C}\label{sec:lw}
Following~\cite{lw}, for functions $f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4$ on $\F_2^{12}$ let
$f_1\cat f_2\cat f_3\cat f_4$ be the function on $\F_2^{14}$ equal to $f_1$,
$f_2$, $f_3$, $f_4$ on the cosets $(x_{13},x_{14})=(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$, so
that its weight is $\sum\wt f_i$. For $A,B\in\RM(6,12)$ we have
$0\cat A\cat B\cat(A+B)=x_{13}A+x_{14}B\in\RM(7,14)$. Every codeword
$g=0\cat(g_1+a_1)\cat g_2\cat(g_1+g_2+a_2)$ of Construction~2 with $m=6$ equals
$x_{13}(g_1+a_1)+x_{14}g_2+(a_1+a_2)x_{13}x_{14}$, so it lies in $\RM(7,14)$.
If Conjecture~2 held for $m=6$, the weights of these codewords would include
$322$, $326$, $330$, $334$ and $354$, which lie in its first family.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~C]
Let $g_1=x_{T_0}+x_{S_1}+x_{S_2}$ and $g_2=x_{T_0}+x_{S_3}+x_{S_4}$, where
$T_0=\{1,\dots,6\}$ and $S_1,\dots,S_4$ are the sets of indices in the four
products, so that $1\le|S_j|\le6$, and let $a_1,a_2\in\F_2$ and
$g=0\cat(g_1+a_1)\cat g_2\cat(g_1+g_2+a_2)$, with
\[
\wt g=\wt(g_1+a_1)+\wt g_2+\wt(g_1+g_2+a_2).
\]
If $a_1=1$ and $a_2=0$, then $\wt g=(4096-\wt g_1)+\wt g_2+\wt(g_1+g_2)\ge4096$,
as $\wt g_2+\wt(g_1+g_2)\ge\wt g_1$; similarly $\wt g\ge4096$ if $a_1=0$ and
$a_2=1$. So let $a_1=a_2=a$ and suppose that $\wt g=322$. The three functions
$g_1+a$, $g_2$ and $g_1+g_2+a$ sum to $0$, so each of their weights is at most
the sum of the other two, hence at most $161$. If $a=1$, choose $i\in S_1$
and $j\in S_2$, and put $Q=(1+x_1)(1+x_i)(1+x_j)$. This polynomial has
degree at most~$3$, is nonzero at the zero vector, and its support is
contained in that of $g_1+1$. By (P3), $\wt(g_1+1)\ge\wt Q\ge2^{12-3}=512>161$,
a contradiction. So $a=0$, and $g_1$, $g_2$ and
$g_1+g_2=x_{S_1}+x_{S_2}+x_{S_3}+x_{S_4}$ are sums of at most four monomials of
degree at most $6$ in $x_1,\dots,x_{12}$, of weight at most $161$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:ie} the weight of such a sum depends only on the sizes of the
Venn regions of its index sets, and an exhaustive check of the feasible sizes
shows that every weight at most $161$ of such a sum belongs to the set
\[
0,64,96,112,120,124,126,128,136,144,148,152,154,156,158,160
\]
(this necessary inclusion is verified in Lean; Section~\ref{sec:lean}). Write
$a\le b\le c$ for the three weights; then $a+b+c=322$, and $c\le160$. The
listed numbers that are $2$ modulo $4$ are $126$, $154$ and $158$, and
$322\equiv2\pmod4$, so an odd number of $a,b,c$ are among them; as three of them
sum to at least $378$, exactly one is. But none of $322-126=196$,
$322-154=168$ and $322-158=164$ is a sum of two elements of
$\{0,64,96,112,120,124,128,136,144,148,152,156,160\}$. So no codeword of
Construction~2 with $m=6$ has weight $322$, whereas Conjecture~2 of~\cite{lw}
asserts that $2^8+2^6+2=322$ is one.
\end{proof}

\section{Partial non-existence results for the open weights}\label{sec:nonex}

\subsection{Sums of flats}
Call two flats \emph{transversal} if they meet in exactly one point. Two
transversal flats of $\F_2^{14}$ of dimension at least $7$ both have dimension
$7$: if their direction spaces are $V$ and $V'$, then $V\cap V'=\{0\}$, so
$\dim V+\dim V'\le14$.

\begin{propD}
Let $F_1,F_2,F_3$ be flats of $\F_2^{14}$ of dimension at least $7$, not
necessarily distinct (the whole space is allowed). Then none of $\wt 1_{F_1}$,
$\wt(1_{F_1}+1_{F_2})$ and $\wt(1_{F_1}+1_{F_2}+1_{F_3})$ lies in $U$.
\end{propD}
\begin{proof}
The weight of $1_{F_1}+1_{F_2}+1_{F_3}$ is
$\sum|F_i|-2\sum_{i<j}|F_i\cap F_j|+4|F_1\cap F_2\cap F_3|$, and a nonempty
intersection of flats has a power of $2$ as its size. As $4\mid|F_i|$, the
weight is $2$ modulo $4$ if and only if an odd number of the three pairs are
transversal. The same holds for one or two flats. All elements of $U$ are $2$
modulo $4$.

For one flat there is no transversal pair. Two transversal $7$-flats give the
weight $128+128-2=254$. Three pairwise transversal $7$-flats give
$384-6+4|F_1\cap F_2\cap F_3|\in\{378,382\}$. It remains to treat exactly one
transversal pair, say $F_1\cap F_2=\{p\}$ with $\dim F_1=\dim F_2=7$, and
$\dim F_3=d\in[7,14]$. Translate so that $p=0$; then $F_1=V_1$ and $F_2=V_2$
are linear subspaces with $V_1\oplus V_2=\F_2^{14}$. Let $V_3$ be the direction
space of $F_3$. The pairs $(F_1,F_3)$ and $(F_2,F_3)$ are not transversal.

\emph{Case $p\in F_3$.} Then $F_3=V_3$, $|V_1\cap V_3|=2^a$ and
$|V_2\cap V_3|=2^b$ with $a,b\ge1$, and the weight is
$258+2^d-2^{a+1}-2^{b+1}$. Since $(V_3\cap V_1)\oplus(V_3\cap V_2)\subseteq V_3$,
we have $a+b\le d$. The only solutions of $258+2^d-2^{a+1}-2^{b+1}\in U$ with
$1\le a\le b\le7$ and $7\le d\le14$ are $(w,d,a,b)=(322,7,4,4)$ and
$(322,8,5,6)$, and both violate $a+b\le d$.

\emph{Case $p\notin F_3$.} Then the weight is $254+2^d-2(\alpha+\beta)$, where
$\alpha=|F_1\cap F_3|$ and $\beta=|F_2\cap F_3|$ are $0$ or powers of $2$ from
$2$ to $128$. The
only solution in $U$ is $w=334$, $d=7$, $\{\alpha,\beta\}=\{8,16\}$. Then
$\dim(V_3\cap V_1)+\dim(V_3\cap V_2)=3+4=7=\dim V_3$, so
$V_3=(V_3\cap V_1)\oplus(V_3\cap V_2)$. Write $F_3=c+V_3$ and $c=c_1+c_2$ with
$c_i\in V_i$. As $F_3$ meets $F_1$, $c\in V_1+V_3=V_1+(V_3\cap V_2)$, so
$c_2\in V_3\cap V_2$; similarly $c_1\in V_3\cap V_1$. Hence $c\in V_3$ and
$0=p\in F_3$, a contradiction.

Both statements about solutions are finite checks; by hand they are quick, as
$2^{a+1}+2^{b+1}$ and $\alpha+\beta$ have at most two binary digits $1$.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is dimension counting: in $\RM(6,12)$ the indicators of the
$6$-flats spanned by $e_1,\dots,e_6$, by $e_7,\dots,e_{12}$ and by
$e_1,e_2,e_3,e_7,e_8,e_9$ sum to a codeword of weight $162=2.5\cdot64+2$,
whereas $322$ in $\RM(7,14)$ would need $a=b=4$, against $a+b\le7$.

\subsection{Sums of few monomials}
\begin{propE}
No sum of at most five monomials of degree at most $7$ in $x_1,\dots,x_{14}$
(the constant $1$ allowed) has weight in $U$. The same holds for $f\circ\phi$ for
every such sum $f$ and every affine bijection $\phi$ of $\F_2^{14}$.
\end{propE}
\begin{proof}
The second statement follows from the first, as $\phi$ preserves weights. Let
$f=\sum_{i=1}^kx_{S_i}$ with $k\le5$ and $|S_i|\le7$; equal monomials cancel in
pairs, and the constant is $x_\emptyset$. In the formula of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ie} with $m=14$, the terms with $|T|\ge3$ are divisible by $4$;
the term of $T=\{i\}$ is $2^{14-|S_i|}$, divisible by $2^7$; and the term of
$T=\{i,j\}$ is $-2\cdot2^{14-|S_i\cup S_j|}$, which is $2$ modulo $4$ exactly
when $S_i\cup S_j=\{1,\dots,14\}$, that is, when $S_i$ and $S_j$ are
complementary sets of size $7$. Hence $\wt f\equiv2N\pmod4$, where $N$ is the
number of pairs $\{i,j\}$ for which $S_i$ and $S_j$ are complementary sets of
size $7$. All elements of $U$ are $2$ modulo $4$, so if $\wt f\in U$, then $N$
is odd; in particular two of the sets, say $S_1=C$ and
$S_2=\{1,\dots,14\}\setminus C$, are complementary sets of size $7$, and at
most three sets $S_3,\dots,S_k$ remain. Splitting each union according to $C$
and its complement, Lemma~\ref{lem:ie} gives
\[
\wt f=254+\sum_{\emptyset\ne T\subseteq\{3,\dots,k\}}(-2)^{|T|-1}
\bigl(2^{7-p_T}-2\bigr)\bigl(2^{7-q_T}-2\bigr),
\]
where $p_T$ and $q_T$ are the numbers of elements of $\bigcup_{i\in T}S_i$ in
$C$ and in its complement. The right-hand side depends only on the sizes of the
Venn regions of the traces of $S_3,\dots,S_k$ on $C$ and on its complement,
and these satisfy $p_{\{i\}}+q_{\{i\}}=|S_i|\le7$; going through all possible sizes shows
that the right-hand side is never in $U$ (this enumeration is part of the Lean
proof; Section~\ref{sec:lean}).
\end{proof}

Among these sums, the weights $2$ modulo $4$ in $[250,400]$ are $254$, $314$,
$318$ and all such numbers from $338$ to $398$. Version~1 proved
Proposition~E by enumerating all vectors of Venn region sizes $(n_T)$,
$\emptyset\ne T\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}$, with $\sum_Tn_T\le14$ and
$\sum_{T\ni i}n_T\le7$ for all $i$, for $k\le5$, and reported their
numbers; that enumeration is not part of version~2.

\subsection{A reduction}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dir}
Let $f\in\RM(7,14)$ have support $S$ and weight $w\equiv2\pmod4$. For
$a\in\F_2^{14}\setminus\{0\}$ let $n_a$ be the number of pairs $\{x,x+a\}$
contained in $S$. Then $\sum_{a\ne0}n_a=\binom w2$, every $n_a$ is odd, and in
coordinates in which $a$ is the last unit vector we have $f=g+x_{14}h$ with
$g\in\RM(7,13)$, $h\in\RM(6,13)$, $n_a=|\supp g\setminus\supp h|$ and
$\wt h=w-2n_a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each $2$-subset $\{x,y\}$ of $S$ is counted once, by $a=x+y$. Composing $f$
with an invertible linear map sending $e_{14}$ to $a$, we may assume
$a=e_{14}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:halves} with
$m=13$ and $r=6$, $f=g+x_{14}h$ with $g=f(\cdot,0)\in\RM(7,13)$ and
$h=f(\cdot,0)+f(\cdot,1)\in\RM(6,13)$; the pairs in direction $e_{14}$
correspond to the points of $\supp g\cap\supp(g+h)=\supp g\setminus\supp h$,
and $\wt h=w-2n_a$. By (P2), $4\mid\wt h$, so $2n_a\equiv w\equiv2\pmod4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{propF}
Let $w\in U_0$. Then $w$ is a weight of $\RM(7,14)$ if and only if there are
$h\in\RM(6,13)$ and $g\in\RM(7,13)$ with
\[
(\wt h,\ |\supp g\setminus\supp h|)=(w-2,1)\quad\text{or}\quad(w-6,3).
\]
Moreover, a codeword of weight $w=322,326,330,334$ has such a decomposition, as
in Lemma~\ref{lem:dir}, in at least $7559$, $7235$, $6908$, $6576$ directions.
\end{propF}
\begin{proof}
If $g$ and $h$ are as stated, then $f=g+x_{14}h\in\RM(7,14)$ has weight
$\wt h+2|\supp g\setminus\supp h|=w$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:halves}. Conversely,
let $f$ have weight $w$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dir} the $n_a$ are odd and
$\sum_{a\ne0}(n_a-1)=\binom w2-16383$. A direction with $n_a\ge5$ contributes
at least $4$ to this sum, so at most $\lfloor(\binom w2-16383)/4\rfloor$
directions have $n_a\ge5$; for $w=322,326,330,334$ this bound is $8824$,
$9148$, $9475$, $9807$. In each of the remaining directions $n_a\in\{1,3\}$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:dir} gives the decomposition with $\wt h=w-2n_a$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{How the proofs work.}
The formal proofs follow the written ones, with the following differences.
\emph{Preliminaries.} For (P1), the weight modulo~$2$ is written as the sum of
all values of the polynomial, which is the sum of the coefficients of the
monomials in which every variable occurs. (P2) is proved for every polynomial
from Lemma~\ref{lem:ie}, applied to its monomials with repetitions: each term
$(-2)^{k-1}2^{m-u}$ has $u\le rk$ and $u\le m$, which gives the required
power of~$2$. The minimum distance (\texttt{MinDist}) is proved by induction on
$m$ with Lemma~\ref{lem:halves}, with a codeword of weight $2^{m-r}$ built
recursively. For (P3), if a codeword of minimum weight is nonzero at $x$ and
at $x+a$, a nonzero difference of its two halves in the direction $a$ would
violate the bound on the minimum distance, so translation by $a$ preserves
the support; after translating one point of the support to zero, the
support is a linear subspace, and the indicator of the resulting flat is a
product of affine forms (\texttt{FlatBasics}). Lemma~\ref{lem:ie} is proved by expanding the
exclusive or and summing over the points, and Lemma~\ref{lem:halves} by
splitting off the last variable.
\emph{Theorems~A and~B.} The codewords of Table~\ref{tab:wit} are the lists of
monomials of version~1, given as bit masks. The ten rows are checked by $99$
small kernel certificates, which include all $49$ entries of the
$\sigma$-columns together with their structural and weight data;
Lemma~\ref{lem:ie} connects the finite sums to the actual weights. The
$7901$ codewords of Theorem~B are checked in $65$ blocks of at most $256$
weights, each list of monomials being checked for its degree, its number of
monomials and its weight by a kernel certificate, and the blocks are shown to
cover $S_0$. The middle interval is counted by the injection $n\mapsto320+2n$
for $0\le n<7873$; adding the two disjoint sets of eighteen end values and
removing the eight elements of $U$ gives $|S_0|=7901$. The comparison with
$M\setminus U$ and with the set of~\cite[Proposition~4]{la} uses a symbolic
partition of the intervals and their complements, with the remaining small
finite facts checked in the kernel.
\emph{Theorem~C.} The sums of at most four monomials of degree at most~$6$ in
twelve variables are classified by the sizes of their Venn regions, and the
weights at most $161$ are collected into the list of sixteen values of the
written proof. The cases $a_1\ne a_2$ use the four block weights and the
triangle inequality as in the written proof. In the case $a_1=a_2=1$, both the
written and the formal proof use a nonzero polynomial of degree at most~$3$
whose support lies in that of $g_1+1$. The minimum distance of $\RM(3,12)$
gives $\wt(g_1+1)\ge2^9=512>161$, a contradiction.
\emph{Propositions~D, E and~F.} For Proposition~D, the inclusion--exclusion
for indicators, the sizes of flats and the constraint modulo~$4$ reduce to
one transversal pair; the dimensions of directions and of affine spans bound
the sizes of the intersections, a finite integer certificate in the kernel
handles the remaining cases, and the other transversal configurations are
excluded by parity and arithmetic. For Proposition~E, the necessity modulo~$4$ gives the
complementary pair of monomials of degree~$7$; the remaining at most three
monomials are described by the joint Venn regions of their traces on the two
blocks of seven variables, the finitely many cases are checked by kernel
certificates against the weight formula, and affine invariance
(\texttt{AffineInvariance}) gives the second statement. For the remark after
Proposition~E, the nineteen weights are given by explicit sums of at most five
monomials (for $362$,
$\sa+\sbb+x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5x_8x_9+x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5x_{10}x_{11}+x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5x_{12}x_{13}$),
and the other numbers congruent to $2$ modulo~$4$ in $[250,400]$ are excluded
by the interval bound of the proof of Proposition~E.
Lemma~\ref{lem:dir} uses a linear change of coordinates for each direction and
Lemma~\ref{lem:halves}; the identity $\sum_{a\ne0}n_a=\binom w2$ is an
exchange of finite sums, the parity of $n_a$ comes from (P2), and
Proposition~F follows as written, with the four bounds checked by arithmetic.
\emph{Other statements.} Remark~\ref{rem:direct} interpolates an arbitrary
function of seven variables by a polynomial and decomposes the weight of a
direct sum over the product of the two blocks. The weights $162$, $166$, $170$
and $174$ of $\RM(6,12)$ are those of four explicit polynomials of degree~$6$,
checked on their $4096$-point truth tables in blocks. The statement

\paragraph{Faithfulness and non-claims.}
The set $K$ is data: no statement says that it is the set of weights
below~$320$. The statement \texttt{CarletEquiv} concerns our reading of
Carlet's question, stated before the Corollary. The formal Theorem~C concerns
Construction~2 as written in~\cite{lw}: it says that no codeword of it with
$m=6$ has weight $322$, and that Conjecture~2 for $m=6$, formalised with both
of its families, is false. ``Sums of at most
$k$ monomials of degree at most $r$'' are polynomials with at most $k$ terms
and total degree at most $r$; the constant is allowed. In Lemma~\ref{lem:dir},
``coordinates in which $a$ is the last unit vector'' means: for every linear
bijection $\phi$ of $\F_2^{14}$ with $\phi(e_{14})=a$. The statement
\texttt{FactStar} is the fact $(\ast)$ used by Proposition~C$'$ of version~1
(if $G,E$ are flats of $\F_2^{12}$ and $q$ has degree at most~$2$, then
$|G\cap E\cap\supp q|$ is even or at most~$3$); it was kept and proved,
although no proof of version~2 uses it. The statement
\texttt{Sec7Bridge} says that the weight predicate of version~1,
\texttt{RM714.IsRM714Weight}, agrees with the weights of $\RM(7,14)$ defined
here; it carries no bound on the number of monomials, which \texttt{ThmA}
does. The enumerations in the written proofs of Theorem~C and Proposition~E are
carried out inside the Lean proofs; the other steps of the written proofs are
verified only through the results they prove.
