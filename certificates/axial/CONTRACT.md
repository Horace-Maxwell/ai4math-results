## 2. Fixed reading

- **Algebra**: commutative, not necessarily associative or unital, over a field; product bilinear.
- **Fusion law**: any $\cF\subseteq\F$ with $\ast$ ([GS]/[MSZ]); our laws are moreover finite and symmetric ([KMS], [MS]).
- **Axis / primitive / axial algebra**: as quoted; $X$ is *a* generating set of primitive axes (all sources stress that $X$ is part of the data). Our $X$ are irredundant (no proper subset generates).
- **Ideal**: subspace $I$ with $AI\subseteq I$. **Block** $I_a$: smallest ideal containing $a$. **Dominance**: $I_b\subseteq I_a$; "non-symmetric" = some $I_b\subsetneq I_a$ with $a,b\in X$.
- **Decomposable** ([MSZ] 3.3, [GS] l. 591): $A$ is the sum of its proper ideals. For a block $I_a$ (Problem 3.8) the ideals are those of $I_a$ *as an algebra* ([MSZ] l. 373–377 makes clear this is the intended reading).
- **Sum decomposition** ([MS] 3.11, [KMS] 5.1): pairwise annihilating subalgebras generating $A$. **Finest sum decomposition = components**: [MS]'s operational form $B_iB_j=0$ for distinct components.

**Readings of Problem 3.11 / Question 9.5, for arbitrary (finite symmetric) fusion laws** (all are covered by Example S below):
- (R1) [MS] operational form: $\lla X_i\rra\lla X_j\rra=0$ for distinct components.
- (R2) "finest (direct) sum decomposition": if $A$ has only the trivial sum decomposition then Δ is connected.
- (R3) [MSZ]'s reformulation: an indecomposable (not a sum of proper ideals) primitive axial algebra has connected Δ.
- (R4) the general-law analogue of [GS] 3.12: a simple primitive axial algebra has connected Δ. (R4) is a weaker consequence of the conjecture, not a reading of it.
A counterexample to (R4) refutes (R3) and (R2); one simple counterexample therefore disproves all four (review: (R1) ⇒ (R2) ⇒ (R4) and (R1) ⇒ (R3) ⇒ (R4)).

**Strong vs weak readings.** All questions are stated for general ("primitive axial algebra", no restriction on the fusion law) — this is the literal (weak-hypothesis) reading and the one our examples answer. The *restricted* readings — Seress fusion laws, graded laws, Monster type $\cM(\alpha,\beta)$ ([KMS] Conj. 6.2, MathDB #340561), Majorana algebras ([GS] 3.12) — are **not** addressed and remain open. For Jordan type $\cJ(\eta)$ the conjecture is a theorem ([HSS, Thm A]).

## 3. Admissibility of our fusion laws

| law | set | table | [GS]/[MSZ] | [MS] (symmetric) | [KMS] (finite, symmetric) | Seress | graded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\cJ^+(\eta)$ | $\{1,0,\eta\}$ | $\cJ(\eta)$ except $0\ast0=0\ast\eta=\{1,0,\eta\}$ | yes | yes | yes | **no** | only trivially ($0\ast0\ni1,0,\eta$ forces all in $\cF_+$) |
| $\cJ^\circ(\eta)$ | $\{1,0,\eta\}$ | $\cJ(\eta)$ except $0\ast0=\{0,\eta\}$, $0\ast\eta=\{0,\eta\}$ | yes | yes | yes | **no** | only trivially |
| $F_{D3}$ (Peng/KMP) | $\{0,1,2\}$ | $0\ast0=\{0,1\}$, $1\ast2=2\ast2=\{2\}$ | yes | yes | yes | no | — |

In each case $1\ast1=\{1\}$, $1\ast0=\emptyset$, $1\ast\lambda=\{\lambda\}$, as [KMS] l. 252–256 says is automatic for primitive axes; $0\in0\ast0$, the condition [MSZ] Lemma 2.12 needs for direct sums. None of the sources imposes Seress, gradedness, Frobenius forms or anything else in the definitions used by the questions. **Caveat stated honestly:** [KMS] restricted their conjecture to Monster type after explicitly raising the general question; [MS] and [MSZ]/[GS] then stated it for general primitive axial algebras. Specialists will probably regard counterexamples with non-Seress, ungraded laws as showing that the general form needs a hypothesis on the fusion law, not as touching the Monster/Majorana case.

Peng does not mention Problem 3.9 / Question 9.3 (searched the whole source: no "block", "dominance", "3.9", "9.3"). So [GS] 3.9 has in effect been answered by a published example (Aug 2026, and earlier by the KMP classification), but nobody has said so. We record this with credit and do **not** claim 3.9 as new.
- Δ of Peng's algebra is connected ($ab\neq0$); it says nothing about 3.8 or 3.11.
- G1 (`g1/G1-REPORT.md` §(iii)) found the same phenomenon earlier: in the 2-dimensional classification of Kaygorodov–Martín González–Páez-Guillán (arXiv:2211.00334, Nov 2022; J. ## 5. Results (statements; proofs and checks in PROOF.md, Lean statements in STATEMENTS.md)

Notation: $3C(\eta)$ is the Matsuo algebra on $a,b,x$: $ab=\frac\eta2(a+b-x)$, $ax=\frac\eta2(a+x-b)$, $bx=\frac\eta2(b+x-a)$, $a^2=a$, $b^2=b$, $x^2=x$.

- **Theorem A (Example S, main).** $S=3C(\tfrac12)\oplus\mathbb Q c$ with $c^2=c$, $ca=cb=0$, $cx=\tfrac12(x-a-b)-\tfrac14c$, $X=\{a,b,c\}$, is a 4-dimensional primitive $\cJ^+(\tfrac12)$-axial algebra over $\mathbb Q$ which is **simple**, while Δ(X) has two components $\{a,b\}$, $\{c\}$. Hence (R1)–(R4) all fail: [MS] Conj. 3.16 and [GS] Problem 3.11 / [MSZ] Question 9.5 have negative answers for general fusion laws, and the analogue of [GS] 3.12 for general laws fails.
- **Theorem B (Example E, the scout's example).** $E=3C(\tfrac13)\oplus\mathbb Q c$ with $c^2=c$, $ca=cb=0$, $cx=\tfrac13(x-a-b)$, $X=\{a,b,c\}$: primitive $\cJ^+(\tfrac13)$-axial, $I_a=I_b=\langle a,b,x\rangle\subsetneq I_c=E$ (non-symmetric dominance, [GS] 3.9), $E$ indecomposable, Δ disconnected.
- **Theorem C (Example D).** $D=\langle a,b,x,c,d\rangle$, $3C(\tfrac12)$ on $a,b,x$, $c^2=c$, $ca=cb=0$, $cx=d$, $cd=\tfrac12d$, $d^2=0$, $da=db=dx=0$, $X=\{a,b,c\}$: primitive $\cJ^\circ(\tfrac12)$-axial, and the block $I_a=\langle a,b,x,d\rangle$ is the sum of its proper ideals $\langle a,b,x\rangle$ and $\langle d\rangle$ — **a decomposable axial block** ([GS] 3.8 / [MSZ] 9.2 negative for general laws). ($\langle a,b,x\rangle$ is an ideal of $I_a$ but not of $D$, exactly the phenomenon anticipated in [MSZ] l. 373–377.)
- **Remark P.** Peng's algebra: $I_b\subsetneq I_a$ (credit Peng, KMP).
